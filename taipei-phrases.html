Tones. The accent marks are the four tones: ā high and flat, á rising like a question, ǎ dipping, à falling sharply. Get them wrong and people will still understand you in context, especially if you point.

Numbers

tap one to hear it

Prices are said as they’re written. NT$150 is 一百五 (ee bye woo), NT$80 is 八十 (bah shir). For two of something use 兩 (lyahng): 兩個, two of them. Stallholders will often just show you the number on a calculator or their phone.

Have you eaten? · the Taiwanese “how are you”
Why bother? Most people in Taipei speak Mandarin, but many older Taiwanese grew up speaking Hokkien at home. A doh-shyah at a night-market stall usually gets a big smile. The ▶ voice only does Mandarin, so these have no button.

Taiwan writes traditional characters (謝謝, not 谢谢); everything here is in them. The read-aloud uses your phone’s own voices, so it sounds best on a phone with a Chinese (Taiwan) voice installed.
