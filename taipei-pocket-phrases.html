Taipei · 5–12 November

Pocket phrases

The eight you’ll actually use, said the way people in Taipei say them. Read the green line out loud. Close is good enough.

hear it 你好 tap to show it big

Anywhere

Hello

nee how

nǐ hǎo

You’ll hear

歡迎光臨hwan-ying gwang-lin

“Welcome!” Every shop calls it out when you walk in. No need to answer.

Thank you

shyeh-shyeh

xiè xie

Say it for everything: the change, the bag, the door held open.

Excuse me

boo how ee-suh

bù hǎo yì si

To squeeze past on the MRT, or to wave a server over. Also covers a small “oops, sorry”.

Sorry

dway-boo-chee

duì bu qǐ

For a real bump or a spilled drink. For little things, “boo how ee-suh” is what people say.

Goodbye

bye-bye

bāi bāi

Same as English, and it’s what everyone in Taipei says.

At the restaurant door

Three people

sahn way

sān wèi

Hold up three fingers as you say it.

They’ll ask first

幾位？jee way?

“How many?”

No reservation

may-yo ding way

méi yǒu dìng wèi

Or just “may-yo” (no) when they ask.

They’ll ask

有訂位嗎？yo ding way mah?

“Do you have a reservation?”

We have a reservation

yo ding way

yǒu dìng wèi

Then say the name it’s under, and the time if they look unsure. Use it at Le Palais and Mala.
