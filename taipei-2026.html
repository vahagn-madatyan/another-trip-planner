Base camp

One hotel the whole time, which is the right call for Taipei — the city is small enough that nothing worth doing is more than about 40 minutes from the front door.

Nearest MRT Technology Building · ~7 min walk. Da’an is ~15 min and puts you on the Red line.

What’s in the building

  • Shang Palace — Cantonese, dim sum at lunch
  • Ibuki — Japanese teppanyaki
  • Marco Polo Lounge up on 38 — the skyline view without paying for an observatory ticket
  • Outdoor rooftop pool and the spa
  • Far Eastern Sogo department store attached at the base, with a basement food hall

Why the location works

  • Da’an is the leafy, residential middle of the city — quiet at night, walkable
  • Tonghua (Linjiang St) night market is a 15 min walk — the local one, not the tourist one
  • Taipei 101 and Xinyi are ~10 min by cab
  • Yongkang Street food strip is one short ride west
Three of us, one room? Worth confirming the bedding now rather than at check‑in — most rooms here are a king or twins, and a third person usually means a rollaway or connecting rooms. Also flag arrival times to the hotel so the room is ready; standard check‑in is 3 PM and we may all land in the morning.

The eating list

starter set — to be expanded

Taipei’s reputation is built on cheap food rather than expensive food, so most of this costs a few dollars. The one thing worth acting on now is the reservations note below it.

Beef noodle soup

牛肉麵

The city’s signature dish and the one to eat more than once — braised or clear broth, shin meat, pickled mustard greens. Lin Dong Fang and the Yongkang Street shops are the usual arguments.

NT$150–300

Xiao long bao

小籠包

Din Tai Fung started here and the original Xinyi Road branch still runs the queue‑and‑buzzer system. Worth doing once even though it’s a chain now.

NT$250–450

Taiwanese breakfast

豆漿、蛋餅

Hot or cold soy milk, fried dough sticks, egg crepes, turnip cakes. Fuhang Dou Jiang is the famous one with the famous line — there are a hundred unfamous ones that are nearly as good.

NT$50–120

Night market rounds

夜市

Pepper buns, gua bao, oyster omelette, scallion pancake, stinky tofu if anyone’s brave. Eat one thing per stall and keep moving — that’s the whole technique.

NT$40–100 a go

Mango shaved ice

芒果冰

Yongkang Street is the epicentre. Early November is past peak mango, so ask what fruit is actually good that week rather than ordering off the photo.

NT$180–280

Tea, properly

老人茶

Either up at Maokong with the city below, or in one of the Dadaocheng tea houses. Bubble tea was invented in Taiwan and is everywhere — this is the other, slower version.

NT$300–800
Book these now, not in November. Taipei’s tasting‑menu restaurants — RAW and Mume are the two names that come up — release tables well in advance and sell out within minutes of opening the window. If a special dinner on Wednesday the 11th is wanted, that decision has to happen in the next week or two. The hotel concierge can also hold a table at Shang Palace, which needs far less notice.

Ground rules

the boring, useful part
Money New Taiwan dollar (NT$). Roughly NT$32 to US$1 — so NT$100 is about three dollars. Cards work in hotels, department stores and chains; night markets, temples and small noodle shops are cash only. Airport ATMs give the best rate.
EasyCard The tap card that runs everything — MRT, buses, YouBike, and paying at 7‑Eleven and FamilyMart. Buy one each at any MRT station or convenience store and load NT$500 to start. MRT fares run NT$20–65 a ride.
Getting around The MRT is clean, signed in English, and covers almost everything on this list. Taxis are plentiful and cheap by US standards — a cross‑town ride is usually under NT$250 — but many drivers don’t read English, which is why the Chinese hotel address is printed above. Uber works too.
Phones Pick up prepaid tourist SIMs or eSIMs in the arrivals hall at Taoyuan — unlimited data for the week runs about NT$500–1,000 and takes ten minutes. Cheaper and simpler than US roaming plans.
Weather & packing Early November is the good season: highs around 26°C / 79°F, lows about 21°C / 70°F, typhoon season over. Still humid, and the northeast monsoon is starting, which means sudden drizzle. Light layers, comfortable shoes, a compact umbrella each. Indoor air conditioning is aggressive — bring one warm layer.
Entry US passports get visa‑free entry to Taiwan for short stays; passports should have comfortable validity beyond the trip. Taiwan uses an online arrival card — worth filling in before flying instead of at the airport.
Plugs Same as the US — 110V, type A/B. No adapters needed, which is a rare gift.
Etiquette No tipping, anywhere. No eating or drinking on the MRT, including water — it’s enforced. Queue properly; everyone else does.
