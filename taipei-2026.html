Ground rules

the boring, useful part
Money New Taiwan dollar (NT$). Roughly NT$32 to US$1 — so NT$100 is about three dollars. Cards work in hotels, department stores and chains; night markets, temples and small noodle shops are cash only. Airport ATMs give the best rate.
EasyCard The tap card that runs everything — MRT, buses, YouBike, and paying at 7‑Eleven and FamilyMart. Buy one each at any MRT station or convenience store and load NT$500 to start. MRT fares run NT$20–65 a ride.
Getting around The MRT is clean, signed in English, and covers almost everything on this list. Taxis are plentiful and cheap by US standards — a cross‑town ride is usually under NT$250 — but many drivers don’t read English, which is why the Chinese hotel address is printed above. Uber works too.
Phones Pick up prepaid tourist SIMs or eSIMs in the arrivals hall at Taoyuan — unlimited data for the week runs about NT$500–1,000 and takes ten minutes. Cheaper and simpler than US roaming plans.
Weather & packing Early November is the good season: highs around 26°C / 79°F, lows about 21°C / 70°F, typhoon season over. Still humid, and the northeast monsoon is starting, which means sudden drizzle. Light layers, comfortable shoes, a compact umbrella each. Indoor air conditioning is aggressive — bring one warm layer.
Entry US passports get visa‑free entry to Taiwan for short stays; passports should have comfortable validity beyond the trip. Taiwan uses an online arrival card — worth filling in before flying instead of at the airport.
Plugs Same as the US — 110V, type A/B. No adapters needed, which is a rare gift.
Etiquette No tipping, anywhere. No eating or drinking on the MRT, including water — it’s enforced. Queue properly; everyone else does.
