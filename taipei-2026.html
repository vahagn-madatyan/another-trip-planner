The week

your list, placed · trains, MRT, walking

Everything you asked for, placed around the closures and linked by train, MRT, the odd bus, and on foot. Green rows are your picks. Plain rows are the temples, museums and markets I’ve added to fill the gaps. A red stripe means there’s something to reserve, and cash only means bring notes.

Why the days fall this way.
  • The beef noodle shop is closed on Mondays, so it goes on Saturday.
  • The Maokong gondola shuts on Mondays for maintenance, so Maokong is Wednesday.
  • Jiufen is packed at weekends, so the day trip is Monday, with Sunday as the rain swap.
  • Both soy milk shops close on Mondays, so the traditional breakfasts are Sunday and Wednesday.
  • The Palace Museum and Confucius Temple close on Mondays, so they’re Tuesday morning, finishing in time to be back at the hotel by about 16:00 with an hour to change for Le Palais.
  • Every evening in town ends at a night market, best first: Raohe, then Shilin, Ningxia and the local Linjiang Street. Monday’s market is Jiufen’s lantern-lit old street.

Getting around with no taxis. The red line is the spine of the week: from Xinyi Anhe it runs without a change to Taipei 101, the memorial hall, Taipei Main, Le Palais, Ningxia, Yuanshan, Shilin and the Palace Museum bus. The brown line from Liuzhangli covers Anju Street, Maokong and the Zoo. Every MRT station has lifts. Trains run from about 06:00 to midnight.
If Monday is a washout, swap it with Sunday. Check the forecast on Saturday night. The day trip runs on Sunday (busier, but dry), and Taipei 101, the hot pot and Shilin move to Monday; call Mala to move the booking. Fu Hang is closed on Mondays, so do it on Tuesday at 6:00 before the museum instead.
Swap-ins if a day runs short or rains. Xingtian Temple (行天宮), where locals come to have their fortunes read; Huashan 1914, the old winery arts park across the road from Fu Hang; Ximending for an evening of youth culture and neon; Elephant Mountain, a steep 20-minute climb for the classic view of 101; Songshan Cultural and Creative Park, an old tobacco factory with a design museum. Sun Yat-sen Memorial Hall is closed for renovation until 2027.

A sit-down Taiwanese dinner? Optional

Every evening now ends at a night market, so a starred Taiwanese dinner would replace one. If you want one anyway, Saturday works best: Fujin Tree is a walk from SunnyHills, and Raohe is one MRT stop from Nanjing Sanmin for dessert. Book ahead; these are from the 2026 guide.

US customs on the way home. Declare all food on the form. Not allowed: anything with meat, including pork floss, jerky, sausages and meat-filled pastries or instant noodles with meat packets; fresh fruit; and pastries with salted egg yolk. Ground rules

the boring, useful part
Money New Taiwan dollar (NT$). Roughly NT$32 to US$1, so NT$100 is about three dollars. Cards work in hotels, department stores and chains; night markets, temples, breakfast shops and small noodle shops are cash only. The full list is under cash or card.
EasyCard The tap card that runs the whole week: MRT, Airport MRT, TRA trains, buses, the gondola, and paying at 7‑Eleven and FamilyMart. Buy one each on arrival and load NT$1,000; the airport trains, the day trip and the gondola add up. Tap on and off on buses too.
Getting around No taxis this trip. The MRT is clean, signed in English and runs from about 06:00 to midnight. Every station has lifts, and the platforms are level with the trains. Google Maps transit directions are accurate here; the Bus+ app shows live bus arrivals. No eating or drinking past the ticket gates, including water: it’s a fine.
Phones Pick up prepaid tourist SIMs or eSIMs in the arrivals hall at Taoyuan: unlimited data for the week runs about NT$500–1,000 and takes ten minutes. Cheaper and simpler than US roaming plans, and the maps need it.
Weather & packing Early November is the good season: highs around 26°C / 79°F, lows about 21°C / 70°F, typhoon season over. Still humid, and the northeast monsoon brings sudden drizzle, more so at Jiufen and Maokong. Light layers, comfortable walking shoes (this plan walks a lot), a compact umbrella each, and one warm layer for the air conditioning.
Entry US passports get visa‑free entry to Taiwan for short stays. Fill in Taiwan’s online arrival card in the three days before you fly rather than on paper at the airport.
Plugs Same as the US: 110V, type A/B. No adapters needed.
Temples All the temples on the plan are free and open early. Walk in through the right-hand door and out through the left; don’t step on the raised threshold. Photos are fine; flash and selfies in people’s faces while they pray are not.
Etiquette No tipping, anywhere. Queue properly; everyone else does. Stand on the right on escalators.
